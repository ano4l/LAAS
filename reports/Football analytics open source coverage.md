# Approved events, not models, complete this dashboard

No open-source model can fill this dashboard from video, but ordinary arithmetic on human-approved events can fill most of it, and that is where the build effort should go. Of the 42 requested analytics, **13 can be calculated today from the approved events the review tool already stores, 3 more need only a pitch location added to each shot and pass, 14 need a season of history or a comparison population, 5 need GPS files the club has not yet supplied, 5 are ordinary report and export software, and 2 (movement and heatmaps) are already calculated from video but measured to be wrong by several metres**. Every one of the 42 has a permissively licensed method or library behind it; the gap is input data, not code. The only way to show the history, comparison, scouting and workload sections with real numbers this year is clearly labelled open demonstration data, chiefly the Wyscout 2017/18 event dataset, the DFL Bundesliga tracking set, SoccerTrack v2 and SoccerMon, all CC BY 4.0. No open event or player data exists for any South African competition. The most popular football AI projects are unusable in a commercial product: the current detector (Ultralytics YOLO) is AGPL-3.0, the best event-spotting models are GPL-3.0 and trained on NDA-gated video, and StatsBomb's free data bars commercial use of anything derived from it. Automatic event detection, validated distances from broadcast clips, a 45-minute half and any form of injury prediction remain out of reach, and the report says so wherever they come up. Licence readings here are a researcher's reading of published terms as of 18 November 2026, not legal advice.

## Thirteen of 42 analytics need only arithmetic on approved events

The prototype has been trying to reach the analytics through the camera, and the measurements show why that route is closed for now. On ten labelled SoccerNet clips the pipeline found 47% of players, placed them 5 to 21 m from their true position and overstated distance by about 1.6 times (project record `docs/testing/SOCCERNET_GSR_2026-11-15.md`). The rule-based candidates added on 18 November proposed one tackle across 17 stored clips. The published state of the art explains this and does not offer a rescue. The only 12-class event model with public weights, the SoccerNet 2025 team baseline built on T-DEED, scores **53.59 mAP@1 on its own test split** and is GPL-3.0 ([sn-teamspotting](https://github.com/SoccerNet/sn-teamspotting)). In the only per-class table found, a model fed near-certified tracking data reaches about 72% precision and recall on passes, 65% on shots and **0.0% on tackles** ([FOOTPASS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16183v1)). No open video dataset or model found labels interceptions, duels, take-on outcomes or pass completion; those classes do not exist in the SoccerNet taxonomy ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). The authors of the largest play-by-play dataset state that current methods mainly assist annotation instead of replacing it ([FOOTPASS abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16183v1)).

The second route is already built. The review workflow stores type, outcome, team, optional player and timestamp, which is the same type-plus-result pairing the standard open event schema SPADL uses; the fields it lacks are start and end coordinates, body part and period ([SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)). Opta's public definitions of tackles won, duels won, interceptions and take-on success contain no location condition, so counts and success rates need nothing more than what a reviewer already enters ([Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)). Expected goals (xG, the chance a shot scores), expected threat (xT, how much a pass or carry raises the chance of scoring), progressive passes and pressing measures such as PPDA all require a location, and season views require history.

The table below gives one row per catalogue entry. "Approved events, now" means the inputs exist in today's review records and only aggregation and display code is missing. Two cautions apply to every such row. Figures cover only the footage a person has reviewed, and clips are capped at 120 seconds, so nothing is yet a match total. And a professional event feed holds about 1,682 events per match, half of them passes and 28% duels, tagged by three operators ([Pappalardo et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6817871/)); complete manual tagging is hours of work per match.

Three patterns in the table drive the plan. First, a location is the single most valuable field the review tool lacks. A click on a pitch diagram for each shot yields xG and shot maps; a start and end click for passes yields xT, progressive passes and pass networks; and a coarse zone tag is enough, by definition, for PPDA, field tilt and high turnovers, although no study has tested how accurate zone-tagged events are for those metrics. A location-only xG is defensible: in KU Leuven's benchmark a logistic regression on distance and angle with a body-part term scored a Brier score of about 0.078 and AUROC of 0.77, almost identical to far richer models ([DTAI blog](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/illustrating-the-interplay-between-features-and-models-in-xg/)).

Second, fourteen rows cannot be honest on club data until matches accumulate, because rankings, percentiles and trends need a reference population. A rolling ten-match window on three matches should read "insufficient matches (3 of 10)", not a number. Third, the open datasets that can stand in are real but distant from the club: the Wyscout set is the 2017/18 season of five European top divisions plus two tournaments ([Pappalardo et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6817871/)), the DFL tracking set is seven German matches ([Bassek et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)), and SoccerMon is two Norwegian women's teams with self-reported injuries ([SoccerMon](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11139986/)). A search found no open event or player-statistics dataset for the Betway Premiership or any other South African competition; the confirmed commercial route is Hudl/Wyscout, whose announced rights deal with the PSL covered 2024-25 and 2025-26, with no renewal notice found ([Hudl](https://hudl.com/blog/premier-soccer-league-forms-rights-collaboration-with-hudl)).

## The best-known football AI code is copyleft, non-commercial or unlicensed

The prototype's legal exposure begins with what it already runs. `pyproject.toml` pins `ultralytics==8.4.178`, and Ultralytics states that its code and every model trained with it are AGPL-3.0 and that "SaaS platforms, APIs, or cloud systems that use YOLO behind the scenes" need an Enterprise licence unless the whole project is open-sourced ([Ultralytics licence](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)). That applies to the hosted prototype today. The analytics layer, by contrast, carries almost no licence risk: kloppy, socceraction, floodlight, databallpy and mplsoccer are BSD or MIT, and unravelsports and PathCRF are MPL-2.0, a file-level copyleft that permits use as an unmodified dependency. A scraper's MIT licence covers the scraper's code only and grants nothing in the data it collects.

Three items in the "safe" column still need a check before commercial reliance. The Wyscout dataset's paper states CC BY 4.0, but one catalogue entry for the same data lists non-commercial use only, and the licence field on the figshare record itself could not be read ([D4Science entry](https://ckan-sobigdata.d4science.org/dataset/public_data_set_of_spatio-temporal_match_events_in_soccer_competitions)). SkillCorner's MIT licence sits on a repository of data, which is unusual, and team and league names are separate rights ([SkillCorner](https://github.com/SkillCorner/opendata)). And SoccerTrack v2's project page says both its MIT code and CC BY 4.0 data "permit commercial use", yet GitHub could not classify the repository's licence file ([SoccerTrack v2](https://atomscott.github.io/SoccerTrack-v2/)). SoccerTrack v2 matters more than any other single asset: ten full amateur matches of fixed 4K panoramic video with per-frame pitch positions and the 12 SoccerNet-style event classes linked to player IDs, which is the stated target camera with commercially usable training and test labels. It ships no pretrained weights and no published baseline scores ([baselines README](https://github.com/AtomScott/SoccerTrack-v2/blob/main/baselines/README.md)).

## Seven stages add five Python packages and three D3 modules

The core backend today depends on FastAPI, Pydantic, OpenCV and NumPy and little else (`pyproject.toml`), and the frontend has no chart library (`apps/web/package.json`), so the plan below keeps the running application that lean. The principle is that heavy analytics libraries run offline, once, to convert open datasets and fit small models, and the application ships the results: a table of demo events, a handful of xG coefficients and an xT grid. socceraction supports Python 3.9 to 3.12 and describes itself as not actively developed ([socceraction](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/socceraction)), which is a second reason to keep it out of the server. Effort figures are this report's rough estimates for one engineer, not sourced measurements.

Stages 1 and 2 come first because they need no new package, no training data and no owner purchase, and because every later metric inherits their event record. The review-only upload in stage 2 is this report's own suggestion: human tagging needs video playback, not detection, so decoupling it from the 120-second GPU limit is the cheapest way to turn clip counts into match totals. The minimum additions to the review tool, in order of metric yield, are period and clock, shot location, pass start and end, shot body part, and pass receiver.

Stage 4 is where the dashboard fills. Fitting xG and xT on the Wyscout set, whose own tooling already supports that provider ([soccer_xg](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/soccer_xg)), keeps the models clear of StatsBomb's restriction. The progressive-pass rule should be Wyscout's, the only definition found with explicit thresholds in metres ([Wyscout glossary](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/progressive_pass/)), and the interface should name whichever definition is chosen, since Opta's and FBref's differ materially. Suitability and ranking outputs should expose each metric's percentile, weight and contribution, which makes them explainable without any machine-learning explainer.

Stage 5 should default to individual-baseline rules (this week's load against the player's own recent mean and spread) with the acute:chronic ratio as an optional view, and should store every speed threshold as a visible setting. The common bands of 19.8 km/h for high-speed running and 25.2 km/h for sprinting are conventions; a systematic review found no consensus on them ([PMC9968809](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9968809)). For stage 6, Chromium printing is the one route that renders the same SVG charts as the screen on the Windows server and in containers alike; WeasyPrint is lighter but needs Pango libraries on the Windows server ([WeasyPrint](https://doc.courtbouillon.org/weasyprint/stable/first_steps.html)).

The demo datasets vary enormously in size. The Wyscout events and SoccerMon's subjective file are small, but each SoccerMon GPS archive is 16 to 31 GB ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10033832)) and SoccerTrack v2 is ten matches of 4K video, so those belong on cloud storage and should be reduced there to per-session summaries before anything reaches the application; the developer machine's disk is close to full.

Stage 7 is deliberately last in priority and parallel in time. A fixed camera removes per-frame calibration as a class of error, because one checked calibration per installation replaces thousands of automatic ones ([SoccerTrack v2 calibration](https://github.com/AtomScott/SoccerTrack-v2/blob/main/docs/calibration.md)). Detection then becomes the hard part: an off-the-shelf YOLOv8 scored 11.2 mAP on wide side-view football and 52.7 after fine-tuning on the target footage ([TeamTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13868v1)), and the best published tracker on full-pitch fisheye video reached only HOTA 0.60 ([GTATrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00484)).

## Ten owner decisions gate the build

Most of the remaining uncertainty is not technical. The decisions below change what gets built, what may be shown and what the product may legally contain, and several of them reverse work if taken late.

| Decision | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1. May clearly labelled open demo data populate sections the club cannot yet fill? | Without it, 14 history rows and 5 workload rows stay "unavailable" until the club has a season of reviewed matches and GPS files | Yes, in a separate, switchable demonstration workspace. Every tile and export names the source, season and licence; demo and club figures are never mixed. Demo data is real data from other matches, not synthetic, so it fits the standing rule if the owner accepts that reading |
| 2. Is the product commercial? | Commercial use rules out the Ultralytics detector without a paid licence, StatsBomb data, FOOTPASS, every SoccerNet-trained checkpoint, BroadTrack and the Koshkina jersey pipeline | Decide before any further fine-tuning. If commercial, buy the Ultralytics licence or migrate to an Apache-2.0 detector |
| 3. What does "complete" mean? | Every tile showing a club value is not achievable this year; every tile genuinely calculated from real data, with honest unavailable states, is | Adopt the second definition in writing |
| 4. Who tags events, and how many? | A full match holds roughly 1,700 to 1,900 on-ball events | Tag all shots, goals, tackles, interceptions and dribbles; decide whether passes and duels are tagged fully or the tiles are marked partial |
| 5. Zone tag or pitch click? | A zone is faster and enough for PPDA and field tilt; xG and progressive passes need metres | Pitch click for shots and passes, zone as fallback |
| 6. Commit to the fixed wide camera? | Movement, heatmap and shape figures only become credible there | Yes; keep the accuracy warning on all broadcast-clip figures |

| 10. Verify three licences | Wyscout on figshare, SkillCorner's data terms, SoccerTrack v2's licence file | A short legal check before any commercial launch |

Decision 1 deserves a counter-argument. Showing European top-flight percentiles beside a South African squad invites exactly the false comparison the standing rule exists to prevent, and a demo tile seen in a screenshot loses its label. The mitigation is structural: demo data lives in its own competition records, a persistent banner marks the workspace, and exports carry the source line in the file itself. If the owner declines, the same code still ships; the affected tiles simply read "unavailable: needs n more reviewed matches".

## What stays impossible or unproven after all of this

Automatic event statistics stay out of reach. The best public checkpoint scores in the low fifties of mAP on the league it was trained on, adding team attribution costs about 13 points even in-domain, and the one study of transfer across leagues called it hard ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1), [Cabado et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/papers/Cabado_Beyond_the_Premier_Assessing_Action_Spotting_Transfer_Capability_Across_Diverse_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)). No result exists for these models on fixed wide-angle footage. Rule-based detection reports detection rates above 90% for most categories, but on provider-grade tracking with a reliable ball, which this pipeline does not have ([Vidal-Codina et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00804v3)). PathCRF, which infers possession from player tracks without the ball, is the most promising open component, but its accuracy was not obtained and it was trained on professional tracking, not on tracks that split each player into 1.9 identities. Model output therefore remains a proposal queue for a human.

Physical metrics from broadcast clips remain unvalidated and should stay behind their warning. A 1.6-times distance overstatement lies far outside any published tolerance. Even an independent test of commercial broadcast-tracking products reportedly found total-distance bias from -21.8% to +24.3% against stadium tracking ([SAGE 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/22150218261445834)), and a validated multi-camera system that measured total distance within 1% still missed high-speed distance by about 9% ([Linke et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7064167)); both figures were read from search excerpts and need re-verification before quotation. Sprint counts, accelerations and metabolic power from single-camera video should not be presented as measurements, and video-derived load must not feed the workload section.

Injury prediction is not available from any source. A systematic review of 204 sports-injury prediction models found 98% at high or unclear risk of bias and none externally validated ([Bullock et al.](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/output/423719/just-how-confident-can-we-be-in-predicting-sports-injuries-a-systematic-review-of-the-methodological-conduct-and-performance-of-existing-musculoskeletal-injury-prediction-models-in-sport)). A cluster-randomised trial of load management guided by the acute:chronic ratio in 482 youth footballers found no reduction in health problems ([Oslo Sports Trauma Research Center](https://klokavskade.no/en/about-us/news/does-load-management-using-the-acutechronic-workload-ratio-prevent-health-problems)). The ratio is contested on statistical and causal grounds, with one group calling for it to be dismissed ([Impellizzeri et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138569)), while a 2025 meta-analysis still finds an association and advises caution ([PubMed 41029871](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41029871/)). The honest product shows load, its components and its change, states the calculation variant, hides ratios until enough history exists, and never outputs a probability.

Several requested items can be calculated but not validated for this club. Percentile ranks against 2017/18 European leagues describe a different era, level and data definition. Peak-age bands of 25 to 27 come from European top leagues ([Dendir](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4045076?lng=en)), and age curves in lower-ranked leagues are described as fairly unexplored ([Säfvenberg et al.](https://www.ida.liu.se/~nikca89/papers/isace24.pdf)). Per-shot xG shifts materially with a couple of metres of location error, and two professional providers' models disagreed on which team had the higher xG in 13% of matches, so xG belongs at team level to one decimal and labelled an estimate ([DTAI blog](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/how-data-quality-affects-xg/)). No sourced minimum-sample rule for rankings was found; the thresholds will be the product's own and should be shown. Retention and recruitment outputs are evidence sheets for an analyst; no validated open model produces such a recommendation.

Finally, three requirements sit outside what open-source code can settle. The 45-minute half remains unmet. Named-player identity across a half has no public benchmark at all. And a current-versus-previous-season comparison has no commercially clean open data behind it, so that tile waits for the club's second season or a licensed feed.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the project's working assumption. The dashboard was being treated as waiting on better models, when the binding constraints are a location field, a roster, a season of reviewed matches and one GPS export file. That shifts the centre of the product from the detector to the review tool and the event record, and it makes the tile state itself (calculated, demonstration, or unavailable with a stated reason and a count of what is missing) a deliverable as real as any metric.

It also aligns three efforts that looked separate. Approved events are at once the source of every match statistic, the only commercially clean training labels for an event model on this club's camera, and the test set that will show when automatic proposals have become good enough to trust. SoccerTrack v2 plays the same triple role for the fixed-camera target. A build that records every human approval and rejection with its provenance is therefore not a stopgap before automation; it is the only path found by which automation on this footage becomes measurable, licensable and eventually true.
