# Offline association beats bigger models everywhere

The largest accuracy gains for this pipeline come from adding offline, whole-clip stages that the current design lacks, not from swapping the detector. Every top SoccerNet Game State Reconstruction system pairs an ordinary detector and online tracker with an offline tracklet split-and-merge pass driven by ReID embeddings, team and jersey number, and that pass is what lifts association scores to 80-85 ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). The same logic applies to the ball (keep many low-confidence candidates, select one physically consistent path offline), to possession (carry team possession through ball flight instead of counting only "ball near foot" frames), and to pitch calibration (solve keyframes, propagate, smooth). Efficiency follows a similar pattern: TensorRT FP16 is worth roughly 1.3-2x on a large detector, but the bigger wins are GPU decode and preprocessing, dropping server-side rendering, and fanning a half out across parallel L4 containers. "Perfect" is not on offer: the best published systems read jersey numbers correctly on 79-88% of broadcast tracklets, spot 12 event classes at about 60-64 mAP, and commercial broadcast-tracking vendors differ from stadium tracking by 1.7-16 m in position and up to ±24% in total distance. Three caveats frame everything below: no source reports timings on an L4, no benchmark covers identity over a full 45-minute half or amateur wide-angle footage, and the current stack (Ultralytics YOLO) plus most public football weights and datasets carry AGPL, GPL, non-commercial or unclear terms that matter for a hosted product. The roadmap at the end orders the work by evidence strength and cost, starting with a labelled evaluation set, because none of the expected gains have been measured on this footage.

## Twenty-two players, hundreds of IDs: the fix is an offline merge stage

### What the winning systems actually do

The 2025 GSR leaderboard is the clearest evidence of where identity accuracy comes from. The top four teams scored GS-HOTA 61.6-63.9 with association scores (GS-AssA) of **79.6-85.3**, against a public baseline of 29.0 GS-HOTA and 61.4 GS-AssA ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). The winner (KIST) ran YOLO-X, the Deep-EIoU tracker with OSNet ReID embeddings, then split tracklets where identity predictions disagreed, merged them by ReID similarity under spatio-temporal and identity constraints, and majority-voted attributes per tracklet ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). The 2024 winner (Constructor Tech) published the most explicit recipe: split tracklets so each holds a single jersey number and team; merge tracklets with the same number when they do not overlap in time, are physically reachable and share a team; merge the remainder by ReID cosine similarity under the same gates; then interpolate. The authors claim this cut tracklet count by 90%, without a supporting table ([From Broadcast to Minimap](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06357v1)). One 2025 entry simply capped identities at 24 (22 players plus two referees) ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)).

The current pipeline runs BoT-SORT without ReID or ByteTrack and has no offline stage, which is consistent with hundreds of IDs per clip. Tracker benchmarks show detection is not the differentiator: on SportsMOT, MOTA sits at 96-98 for every method while association accuracy ranges from 52 (ByteTrack) to 84 ([Selective Mask Propagation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13033v3)). On SoccerNet-tracking with oracle detections, tuned BoT-SORT with ReID reaches **88.4 HOTA versus 85.0 without ReID and 84.0 for ByteTrack** ([Roboflow trackers results](https://trackers.roboflow.com/latest/evaluations/results/)).

The most directly reusable component is **GTA-Link (MIT)**, a tracker-agnostic post-processor that takes MOT-format output and a ReID checkpoint, splits impure tracklets with DBSCAN over embeddings and merges tracklets by averaged cosine distance with spatial constraints ([sjc042/gta-link](https://github.com/sjc042/gta-link)). On SportsMOT it lifts Deep-EIoU from 77.2 HOTA / 79.8 IDF1 to 81.0 / 86.5 ([Selective Mask Propagation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13033v3)), and its authors report 79.4 to 83.1 HOTA on SoccerNet ([GTA paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2024W/MLCSA2024/html/Sun_GTA_Global_Tracklet_Association_for_Multi-Object_Tracking_in_Sports_ACCVW_2024_paper.html)). The fourth-placed 2025 GSR team used exactly BoT-SORT plus GTA ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). The practical design is therefore: turn appearance embeddings on, persist one embedding per detection, and run a CPU-only split/merge with hard gates (no temporal overlap, same team, reachable at a human speed bound, no conflicting numbers) until each team has at most eleven identities. Gating by reachability in pitch metres, as Constructor Tech did by associating in pitch coordinates, becomes available once calibration lands ([From Broadcast to Minimap](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06357v1)).

Mask-propagation trackers post the highest numbers (SAM 3 with Deep-EIoU: 87.2 HOTA, 93.6 IDF1 on SportsMOT) but SAM 3 costs about 0.11 s per frame on an RTX 5090, and McByte runs at 3-5 fps on an A100 ([Selective Mask Propagation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13033v3); [McByte](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01373v1)). That is hours per half on an L4 by extrapolation, so these are not candidates for the main path.

### Team assignment, jersey numbers and names

Team grouping in the top systems is unsupervised clustering per match with assignment **per tracklet, never per frame**. Constructor Tech clusters only athletes within 30 m of the pitch centre into three groups to get clean prototypes, takes goalkeepers from the penalty areas, and assigns by cosine distance; the 2025 winner clusters jersey colour and majority-votes within tracklets; another team balances cluster sizes; another masks out background with silhouettes before computing colour ([From Broadcast to Minimap](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06357v1); [SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). No source compares colour histograms against SigLIP or kit-trained embeddings quantitatively, so the working hypothesis is that fixing fragmentation and voting per merged track will help more than changing the descriptor. That hypothesis is untested.

Jersey numbers are the weakest component even for the best teams. On the SoccerNet jersey benchmark, tracklet-level accuracy runs from **79.3% to 88.3%** for the two leading open recipes: the Koshkina and Elder multi-stage pipeline (legibility classifier, pose-guided torso crop, fine-tuned PARSeq) and Grad's single-stage uncertainty-aware ViT, which aggregates by keeping the lowest-uncertainty prediction per one-second bin ([Grad, CVPRW 2025](https://lukaszgrad.github.io/jnr)). The Koshkina repository is CC BY-NC 3.0, so its code and weights cannot ship in a commercial product ([jersey-number-pipeline](https://github.com/mkoshkina/jersey-number-pipeline)); the licence of Grad's code was not confirmed. Some 2025 teams used vision-language models (LLaMA-3.2-Vision, Qwen2-VL with a zoom tool over a grid of track crops), but no per-component accuracy was published and several teams named jersey numbers as their main weakness ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). Those figures are for broadcast footage with close views; on a wide tactical camera most frames will be illegible, which is why merging must come first so that a handful of readable frames propagate to a long track.

Named players are not something any open project delivers. SoccerNet-GSR identifies athletes by team, role and jersey number, and top systems output "left team number 10", not a name ([SoccerNet GSR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11335)). Mapping to names is a roster lookup the product must build: the user supplies the team sheet, number readings are restricted to numbers actually on the pitch, and the existing review UI gains three operations (merge two tracks, split a track, assign a roster player). Restricting readings to the team sheet is this report's own design suggestion and is not benchmarked anywhere. The realistic ceiling: stable, mostly correct identities on full-pitch tactical footage with human correction of the residual; on broadcast footage, where players leave the frame for long periods and team-mates wear identical kits, no published method demonstrates identity continuity beyond 30-second clips.

## Ball coverage climbs with temporal models and global path selection

The 56-75% ball rate has two causes that the literature addresses separately. The first is single-frame detection. In a controlled re-implementation on soccer footage, single-frame heatmap networks scored F1 of about 45 while every three-frame model scored 85-88, with **WASB at F1 88.3 and about 56 fps on a V100** ([WASB paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.05237)). WASB-SBDT is MIT-licensed and ships soccer weights for seven heatmap trackers ([WASB-SBDT](https://github.com/nttcom/WASB-SBDT)). The caveat is severe: those weights were trained and tested on one game from fixed cameras, so the headline numbers say nothing about other footage, and the model works at 288x512, where a 10 px ball in 1080p shrinks to under 3 px ([WASB paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.05237)). The sensible use is the architecture and training code, run on native-resolution crops around the predicted ball position and fine-tuned on in-domain data.

The second cause is that the ball shares a model with large person classes and the COCO rescue model was never trained on tiny footballs. The best-documented open recipe is Roboflow's dedicated ball detector trained on 2x2 tiles and run with sliced inference, reaching mAP@50 0.925 on its own validation split while its author still reports frequent false negatives ([Roboflow ball tracking](https://roboflow.com/blog/tracking-ball-sports-computer-vision)). No source compares slicing, stride-4 heads, RF-DETR or D-FINE on football ball recall, so that choice has to be settled in-house. SAM 2-class trackers are not the answer: the only evidence is anecdotal and mixed, and SAM-based football tracking runs at about 7.7 fps ([Sieve](https://alpha.sievedata.com/blog/ball-tracking-with-sam2); [arXiv 2512.08467](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08467v1)).

The cheapest step needs no new model. Open ball trackers use weak greedy selection: Roboflow's keeps the detection nearest the mean of the last ten positions and explicitly lacks a distance gate, prediction and interpolation, and WASB's own tracker did not help on soccer ([Roboflow ball tracking](https://roboflow.com/blog/tracking-ball-sports-computer-vision); [WASB paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.05237)). The classical broadcast literature instead builds a graph of candidates across frames and extracts the best path with Viterbi ([Trajectory-based ball tracking](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3424486_Trajectory-Based_Ball_Detection_and_Tracking_in_Broadcast_Soccer_Video)). Because this pipeline is offline, it can keep the top few candidates per frame from every source at very low thresholds, add a "missing" state with a skip penalty, solve for the path, smooth it forward-backward, and interpolate only short gaps while flagging every synthetic point. That design is engineering judgement built on old papers; no 2024-2026 study quantifies it for football.

Ball coverage should also be measured properly before and after. The 56-75% figure counts frames with any candidate, not recall on frames where the ball is visible, so the true headroom is unknown until a labelled set separates visible, occluded and out-of-frame frames.

### Possession coverage of 1-12% is a definition problem as much as a ball problem

The reference rule-based method (Vidal-Codina et al.) defines per-frame control by a 0.5-1 m player-ball radius, a contested "duel" state when two opponents are within 1 m, and confirms a real touch only when the ball's direction or speed changes inside the player's zone; passes are a loss followed by a reception ([Vidal-Codina et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.00804)). Team possession then persists through ball flight between team-mates, so it should cover nearly all in-play time. A system reporting 1-12% is almost certainly counting only proximity frames in pixel space. The fix is a state machine (controlled, contested, in flight, dead, unknown) in pitch metres, with team possession carried forward until an opponent's confirmed touch and "unknown" reported honestly as a data-quality share. This depends on the ball path and on calibration, which is why both precede event work in the roadmap.

## Metres are available at 0.2-0.6 m on clean frames and far worse on video

Speed, distance, heatmaps, formations and pressing only need ground-plane positions, so a homography suffices in principle. The best open single-frame calibrator is **PnLCalib**: mean projection error of 0.60 m on WC14 and 0.23 m on TS-WorldCup with dataset-specific fine-tuning, and JaC@5 of 80.6 on the SoccerNet 2022 centre-camera test set against 54.8 for TVCalib ([PnLCalib paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401)). Two facts temper that. It takes 164-439 ms per frame on a 2080 Ti, which rules out per-frame use on a half ([PnLCalib paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08401)). And single-frame methods degrade on continuous video: on SoccerNet-gamestate sequences the predecessor NBJW scores JaC@5 37.1 and TVCalib 19.9, while the temporal tracker BroadTrack reaches 56.9 ([BroadTrack paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)). BroadTrack's ablation also shows a single radial-distortion coefficient lifting JaC@5 from 42.1 to 54.1, the largest single term ([BroadTrack paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)).

Licences shape the choice. BroadTrack is restricted to non-commercial research ([BroadTrack LICENSE](https://github.com/evs-broadcast/BroadTrack/blob/main/LICENSE)). PnLCalib code is GPL-2.0-or-later with CC BY 4.0 weights, and its README notes the base models were trained on SoccerNet data under a research agreement that has since expired ([PnLCalib](https://github.com/mguti97/PnLCalib)). TVCalib and Spiideo's soccersegcal are MIT ([tvcalib](https://github.com/MM4SPA/tvcalib); [soccersegcal](https://github.com/Spiideo/soccersegcal)). Roboflow's 32-keypoint pitch model is a quick homography baseline, but it is trained on Bundesliga broadcast frames, publishes no benchmark, has no temporal handling, and its YOLOv8 weights are AGPL when self-hosted ([Roboflow calibration](https://roboflow.com/blog/camera-calibration-sports-computer-vision)).

The architecture that follows is the one the 2025 teams converged on: calibrate keyframes at one or two per second, propagate between them with optical flow on pitch pixels with players masked out, smooth camera parameters (not raw homography entries) over time, and attach a validity state to every frame ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1); [From Broadcast to Minimap](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06357)). BroadTrack's re-initialisation rule, a Jaccard overlap below 0.5 between detected markings and the projected template, is the one sourced threshold for refusing output ([BroadTrack paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01721v1)). For a fixed wide tactical camera the problem is much easier: calibrate once per camera with a distortion term and re-check for drift. That footage type should be the first target for anything measured in metres. No public calibration dataset or accuracy figure exists for amateur or low-elevation footage, and unknown pitch dimensions add their own error.

### Realistic ceilings for physical metrics

The only peer-reviewed comparison of broadcast-derived tracking against stadium tracking found three commercial vendors ranging from **1.68 m to 16.39 m position RMSE, 0.34-2.38 m/s speed RMSE, and total-distance bias from -21.8% to +24.3%** ([Crang et al.](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2508.19477)). Even among professional multi-camera systems, total distance agrees with GPS to within about 4-5% but sprint distance differs by 18% against GPS and by 61% between two optical systems ([UCLan study](https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/56538/); [Cardiff Met](https://pure.cardiffmet.ac.uk/en/publications/interchangeability-of-external-player-load-variables-from-differe/)). The defensible product position is therefore tiered: total distance and average speed per observed minute are reasonable after smoothing; speed-zone distances are estimates comparable only within one camera setup; acceleration counts and anything framed as injury prediction are not supportable from this data. On broadcast footage, off-screen imputation exists (a training-free MIT baseline halves pitch-control error) but leaves individual positions 3.3-8.9 m off, so imputed positions must never feed distance or speed ([arXiv 2607.11548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11548)).

Once positions are in metres, the downstream code is largely off the shelf: kloppy (BSD-3) as the data container, unravelsports (MPL-2.0) for Pressing Intensity and EFPI formation detection from tracking alone, mplsoccer (MIT) for heatmaps and pass networks, floodlight and databallpy (MIT) for velocities and space control ([kloppy](https://github.com/PySport/kloppy); [unravelsports](https://github.com/UnravelSports/unravelsports); [mplsoccer](https://github.com/andrewRowlinson/mplsoccer); [floodlight](https://github.com/floodlight-sports/floodlight); [databallpy](https://github.com/Alek050/databallpy)). Formation detection needs nearly all outfield players in view, so it is realistic for tactical cameras and intermittent on broadcast.

## Automatic events are proposal generators, not a finished feed

Two routes exist and they fail in complementary ways. Rules on tracking data reach **over 90% precision and recall for passes** and most set pieces, but only 78% precision and 53% recall for shots, and cannot detect tackles at all; those results assume professional 25 Hz tracking with sub-metre ball position at pass time ([Vidal-Codina et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.00804)). Video spotting does not need a ball track. The 2023 two-class winner (pass and drive, MIT code with weights) scored 86-87 mAP at one-second tolerance ([lRomul/ball-action-spotting](https://github.com/lRomul/ball-action-spotting)), but across twelve classes the 2025 leaders reached only **60-64 mAP@1**, and the 2026 player-attributed task was won at 58.9 macro-F1 by a system fusing per-player video features with tracking-derived game state ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1); [SoccerNet 2026 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.07320)). T-DEED, the 2024 winner and the base of most 2025 entries, publishes checkpoints and a video inference script under GPL-3.0 ([T-DEED](https://github.com/arturxe2/T-DEED)).

All of those numbers are on professional broadcast video. A CVPR workshop study found transfer from professional broadcasts to amateur AI-piloted cameras "challenging" ([Cabado et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/CVsports/papers/Cabado_Beyond_the_Premier_Assessing_Action_Spotting_Transfer_Capability_Across_Diverse_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)), and no benchmark exists for wide-angle single-camera footage. General vision-language models are weaker still: 0.34-0.45 accuracy on SoccerNet question answering, and about 80% top-1 for a fine-tuned classifier on pre-clipped broadcast events ([MSUE](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.12106.md); [MatchVision](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01820v3)). They can serve as a second opinion on candidate clips, nothing more.

The existing human review UI is therefore an asset, not a stopgap. The workable design runs rules and a spotter at low thresholds, ranks candidates by disagreement, stores both approvals and rejections, and fine-tunes the spotter on reviewed matches. The 2025 winner's recipe of large pre-training followed by fine-tuning on a modest set of task games supports this pattern, though how many reviewed matches close the amateur domain gap is unknown ([SoccerNet 2025 results](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19182v1)). With roughly 1,900 on-ball events per match in the FOOTPASS dataset ([FOOTPASS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16183)), review effort should go to shots, goals, set pieces and tackles, with passes sample-audited.

For value models, expected threat is the low-risk start: it needs only passes and carries with start and end locations, and socceraction (MIT) implements it, though the library is no longer actively developed ([socceraction](https://pypi.org/project/socceraction/1.2.3/)). xG needs reliable shots, which is exactly the class both routes handle worst, and the convenient training source, StatsBomb open data, is released for non-commercial use ([statsbomb/open-data](https://github.com/statsbomb/open-data)). xG on amateur footage should be labelled indicative and trained on commercially usable data.

## Throughput is bounded by CPU decode and rendering, not the L4

Ultralytics' own L4 measurements show TensorRT FP16 cutting latency from 7.76 ms to 5.73 ms for an x-size model (1.35x) and from 4.98 ms to 1.03 ms for a nano model (4.8x) at 640 px, with no accuracy change; INT8 loses 3.4-7.6 mAP points, a poor trade for a ball a few pixels wide ([Ultralytics ONNX vs TensorRT](https://www.ultralytics.com/blog/onnx-vs-tensorrt)). A 1280 px football detector behaves like the large case, so the honest expectation is **1.3-2x, not 5x**, and no published 1280 px L4 figure exists. Engines are specific to the GPU and TensorRT version, so they must be built on an L4 and cached, for which a Modal Volume is the documented pattern ([Ultralytics TensorRT docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/tensorrt); [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)).

The larger gain is outside the model. In a third-party A100 benchmark, moving decode and preprocessing to the GPU raised a YOLOv8n pipeline from about 140 fps with OpenCV to 405-459 fps with PyNvVideoCodec, and nearly all of the saving was CPU preprocessing (2,471 ms down to about 100 ms per 1,024 frames) ([Yash Jain benchmark](https://yashjain14.github.io/blogs/pynvvideocodec-vs-dali-vs-opencv-benchmark.html)). The current pipeline adds two more serial CPU stages, overlay drawing and H.264 re-encoding. The Eagle project treats tracking data as the product and annotated video as a debugging aid ([nreHieW/Eagle](https://github.com/nreHieW/Eagle)); following that lead, the frontend can draw overlays on a canvas over the original video and the tracking output can move from one large JSON to chunked columnar files. These output-side recommendations are engineering judgement with no sourced measurements.

The 120-second limit is best removed by chunking. Modal bills an L4 at $0.000222 per second and the Starter plan allows ten concurrent GPUs ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)). By this report's arithmetic, a 67,500-frame half costs about $0.60 of GPU time at 25 fps end-to-end and about $0.25 at 60 fps, so splitting a half into overlapping segments across parallel containers cuts wall-clock time roughly in proportion at similar cost. Stitching tracks across segment boundaries is unproven in any published football pipeline, but the offline merge stage described above is designed for exactly that job. No open football project reports end-to-end throughput on any GPU, so every figure here needs a local benchmark.

On detectors, Roboflow's T4 benchmark puts RF-DETR, D-FINE and LW-DETR 5-9 COCO AP above YOLO11 at equal latency and level with YOLO26, but the comparison comes from an interested party, reports no small-object AP, and uses 384-704 px inputs that say little about 1280 px football frames ([RF-DETR benchmarks](https://rfdetr.roboflow.com/develop/learn/benchmarks/)). The reason to consider them is licensing, not proven accuracy.

## The roadmap: measure first, then association, ball, speed, metres

### Licence position for a hosted product

Ultralytics states that an Enterprise licence is required for SaaS products that use YOLO behind the scenes, and that models trained with its code are AGPL-3.0 by default ([Ultralytics licence](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)). That applies to the current pipeline today, so the choice between buying the licence, open-sourcing the service, or migrating to an Apache-2.0 detector (RF-DETR N-L, D-FINE) should be made before more fine-tuning effort goes into Ultralytics weights; RF-DETR's XL and 2XL models are under a separate non-Apache licence ([RF-DETR benchmarks](https://rfdetr.roboflow.com/develop/learn/benchmarks/)). No independent legal opinion was found, and this report is not one. Elsewhere the pattern is consistent: the clean pieces are GTA-Link, TrackLab, roboflow/sports code, WASB-SBDT code, TVCalib, kloppy, mplsoccer and floodlight; the copyleft pieces are BoxMOT (AGPL-3.0), sn-gamestate and T-DEED (GPL-3.0) and PnLCalib code (GPL-2.0+); the restricted or unclear pieces are the Koshkina jersey pipeline (non-commercial), BroadTrack (non-commercial), PRTReID (Hippocratic License), Deep-EIoU and its sports ReID checkpoint (no licence stated) ([boxmot](https://github.com/mikel-brostrom/boxmot); [sn-gamestate](https://github.com/SoccerNet/sn-gamestate); [prtreid](https://github.com/VlSomers/prtreid); [Deep-EIoU](https://github.com/hsiangwei0903/Deep-EIoU)). SoccerNet dataset terms, widely understood to be research-only under an NDA, could not be confirmed from a primary page, so every SoccerNet-trained checkpoint should be treated as a commercial risk until checked. The durable answer is to retrain the small models (ReID, ball, pitch keypoints, digit reader) on self-labelled footage, which the review UI can generate.

### Prioritised plan

Effort figures are this report's rough estimates for one engineer; expected gains quote benchmark evidence from other footage and are not predictions for this system.

Steps 1 to 3 need no new training data and address the three most visible weaknesses, which is why they come first. Step 4 gates everything measured in metres, and steps 7 and 8 depend on all of the earlier ones.

## Conclusion

The research community has largely stopped trying to make the online pass smarter. Detection is close to saturated on sports benchmarks, and the systems that win treat the first pass as a generator of short, pure fragments and candidates, then resolve identity, ball path and camera with global constraints the game itself supplies: eleven per side, one ball, a camera on a tripod, a team sheet. An offline product processing whole halves is unusually well placed to exploit that, and the costliest research components (VLMs, mask propagation, per-frame calibration) can be applied per tracklet or per keyframe instead of per frame.

The second implication is that the product's review UI and its own footage are its real moat. Public weights are narrow, benchmark numbers are all from professional broadcast clips of 30 seconds, and the licences on the best models and datasets block commercial use. A pipeline that records every human correction as training data for its own ReID, ball, keypoint and digit models will close the amateur-footage gap that no published system has measured, and will do so with clean provenance. Until the gold set exists, every gain in this report is a hypothesis.
