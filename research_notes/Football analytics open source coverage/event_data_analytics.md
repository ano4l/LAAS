# Open-source libraries, models and open datasets for football event-data analytics (as of 18 November 2026)

Research notes. All repository metadata (last push, latest release, licence id) marked "GitHub API" was read from `api.github.com/repos/<owner>/<repo>` on 2026-11-18. Licence readings are a researcher's reading of the published terms, not legal advice.

## 1. Schemas and loaders: SPADL / atomic-SPADL, kloppy, StatsBomb JSON, Wyscout, Common Data Format — which target schema, and which fields does each require?

### Takeaway
SPADL (12 flat attributes, 105 x 68 m pitch) is the smallest schema that socceraction's xT and VAEP consume directly, and the 2025 Common Data Format (CDF) is the vendor-neutral superset (adds receiver, related events, is_successful, centre-origin metric coordinates). A home-grown event record should carry the CDF mandatory event fields and export to SPADL; kloppy (BSD-3, actively released) is the practical bridge for loading every open dataset.

### Cited Findings
- **SPADL definition.** A game is a sequence of on-the-ball actions, each with twelve attributes: game_id, period_id, seconds, player, team, start_x, start_y, end_x, end_y, action_type, result, bodypart. — [socceraction docs: SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)
- SPADL has 22 action types: pass, cross, throw-in, crossed free kick, short free kick, crossed corner, short corner, take-on, foul, tackle, interception, shot, penalty shot, free kick shot, keeper save, keeper claim, keeper punch, keeper pick-up, clearance, bad touch, dribble, goal kick. — [socceraction docs: SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)
- SPADL results are success or fail, with special values offside (passes, corners, free kicks), own goal (shots), yellow card and red card (fouls). Body parts are foot, head, other, none (Wyscout data uses a combined head/other value). — [socceraction docs: SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)
- SPADL coordinates: origin bottom-left, uniform 105 m x 68 m field, home team attacks right; `play_left_to_right()` re-orients so low x is the acting team's own half. Converters are documented for StatsBomb, Wyscout and Opta, and a kloppy converter is listed in the API. Synthetic dribbles inserted between events have no `original_event_id`. — [socceraction docs: SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)
- **Atomic-SPADL.** Eleven attributes: game_id, period_id, seconds, player, team, x, y, dx, dy, action_type, bodypart. The result attribute is removed; a pass is split into pass + receival, failed passes become interception or out, and shots, free kicks and corners are each split into two actions (e.g. shot + goal). — [socceraction docs: Atomic-SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/atomic_spadl.html)
- **socceraction** provides SPADL and atomic-SPADL converters, xT, VAEP and Atomic-VAEP; supports StatsBomb, Opta, Wyscout, Stats Perform and WhoScored data; MIT licence; officially supports Python 3.9–3.12; the README states it is not actively developed and exists mainly for reproducibility of the authors' research. — [ML-KULeuven/socceraction](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/socceraction)
- socceraction maintenance state: latest GitHub release v1.5.3 published 2024-08-15; last push 2026-10-18; licence MIT; not archived. — [GitHub API: ML-KULeuven/socceraction](https://api.github.com/repos/ML-KULeuven/socceraction)
- **kloppy** (version 3.19.1) offers a vendor-independent data model for event and tracking data. Loaders listed: DataFactory, HawkEye, Impect, Hudl Sportscode, Hudl StatsBomb, Metrica Sports, Opta, PFF, SecondSpectrum, Signality, SkillCorner, Sportec Solutions, Stats Perform, TRACAB, Wyscout. — [kloppy docs](https://kloppy.pysport.org/)
- kloppy event types: Generic, Pass, Shot, Take on, Carry, Clearance, Interception, Duel, Substitution, Card, Player on, Player off, Recovery, Miscontrol, Ball out, Foul committed, Goalkeeper, Pressure, Formation change. Events carry coordinates (Point), a Result and Qualifiers (BodyPartQualifier, PassQualifier, DuelQualifier, SetPieceQualifier, others). Coordinate systems are modelled with Origin, VerticalOrientation, PitchDimensions and Orientation, and can be transformed between providers. — [kloppy docs](https://kloppy.pysport.org/)
- kloppy maintenance state: release v3.19.1 published 2026-10-03; last push 2026-11-06; licence BSD-3-Clause. — [GitHub API: PySport/kloppy](https://api.github.com/repos/PySport/kloppy)
- **StatsBomb open-data layout.** `competitions.json`, matches organised by competition id and season id, events and lineups per match, and a `three-sixty` folder with one 360 file per match for selected matches. — [statsbomb/open-data](https://github.com/statsbomb/open-data)
- **statsbombpy** exposes `competitions`, `matches`, `lineups`, `events`, `competition_events`, `frames`, `competition_frames` (360 freeze frames), plus `player_match_stats`, `player_season_stats`, `team_match_stats`, `team_season_stats`. Open data needs no authentication; aggregated stats and 360 metrics are for paying customers. Event tables include `shot_statsbomb_xg` and `shot_statsbomb_xg2` columns. — [statsbomb/statsbombpy](https://github.com/statsbomb/statsbombpy)
- **Wyscout public dataset (v2-style) event fields.** eventId/eventName (seven types: pass, foul, shot, duel, free kick, offside, touch), subEventId/subEventName (e.g. cross, simple pass, air duel, corner, penalty), tags (accurate, not accurate, key pass, assist, goal, cards), eventSec (seconds from start of half), matchPeriod (1H, 2H, E1, E2, P), positions (origin and destination x, y in 0–100 percent of the pitch from the acting team's attacking perspective), plus id, matchId, playerId, teamId. — [Pappalardo et al. 2019, Scientific Data (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6817871/)
- **Common Data Format (CDF).** "Common Data Format (CDF): A Standardized Format for Match-Data in Football (Soccer)", arXiv:2505.15820, first submitted February 2025, v4 August 2025, described as CDF version 1.0.0. It specifies a minimal schema for five data types: match sheet, video footage, event data, tracking data, match meta data. Only match sheet data is always mandatory; event or tracking data also requires meta data. — [arXiv:2505.15820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15820)
- CDF event file format: JSON Lines, one object per line, UTF-8. Mandatory per-event fields: id, time (UTC), period, type, sub_type, is_successful, outcome_type, player_id, team_id, receiver_id, receiver_time, x, y, x_end, y_end, body_part, related_event_ids (null when not applicable). Root level: match id and meta.is_synced. — [arXiv:2505.15820v4 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15820v4)
- CDF optional event fields: tracking frame_id / frame_id_end, match_clock, and metrics xg, post_shot_xg, xpass, epv, packing; VAR flags. — [arXiv:2505.15820v4 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15820v4)
- CDF coordinates: metric units, origin at pitch centre, x along sidelines, y along goal lines, range [-L/2, L/2] x [-W/2, W/2] with pitch length/width in metadata, max three decimals; the home team plays left to right for the whole match; snake_case, British spelling. Body part values: left_foot, right_foot, head, other. Periods: first_half, second_half, first_half_extra, second_half_extra, shootout. — [arXiv:2505.15820v4 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15820v4)
- CDF event types include shot, pass, referee and misc, with sub-types such as penalty_kick, free_kick, corner_kick, throw_in, goal_kick, kick_off, foul, caution, offside, substitution, tackle, chance_without_shot. Mandatory metadata includes competition/season/match ids, kickoff time, periods with play direction, stadium with pitch dimensions, teams, players and data-source versions. The paper is CC BY 4.0. — [arXiv:2505.15820v4 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15820v4)
- An open-source CDF validator (MIT) exists on GitHub and PyPI as `common-data-format-validator`. — [science.ecosyste.ms listing](https://science.ecosyste.ms/projects/59635)
- A 2026 arXiv paper on off-ball defending used CDF for German domestic data and converted World Cup tracking data into CDF. — [arXiv:2606.19931](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19931)
- **databallpy** converts all events to the SPADL vocabulary and exports a socceraction-compatible dataframe via `game.to_spadl()`; it also adds carries between consecutive same-team actions. MIT licence. — [Alek050/databallpy](https://github.com/Alek050/databallpy)

### Inferences
- Most practical target for events created from video review: store one flat record per on-ball action with CDF field names (id, period, time, type, sub_type, is_successful, player_id, team_id, receiver_id, x, y, x_end, y_end, body_part, related_event_ids), in metres on a 105 x 68 pitch, and derive SPADL (start_x/start_y/end_x/end_y/result/bodypart) by a simple origin shift. This keeps socceraction's xT and VAEP usable without a custom converter and matches the same schema the open datasets land in via kloppy.
- The current prototype schema (type, outcome, team, player, timestamp) maps onto SPADL's action_type, result, team, player, seconds; the four missing SPADL attributes are start_x, start_y, end_x, end_y, plus bodypart and period_id. A possession id is not a SPADL or CDF mandatory field — possessions are derived from team sequences.
- Minimum additions with the highest metric yield, in order: (1) period + match clock, (2) start x/y for shots, (3) start and end x/y for passes and carries, (4) body part for shots, (5) receiver id for passes.
- socceraction is usable but frozen in practice (last release August 2024, self-declared not actively developed); pin the version and treat it as a reference implementation rather than a dependency that will track new pandas/Python versions.

### Gaps
- StatsBomb event JSON coordinate system (commonly documented as a 120 x 80 pitch with origin top-left) and the full field list of the StatsBomb event spec PDF were not re-read in this session.
- Wyscout v3 (current commercial API) field differences from v2 were not retrieved; only the public v2-style dataset is documented above.
- Whether kloppy ships a CDF serializer/loader in 2026 could not be confirmed; a targeted search returned no kloppy release note mentioning CDF. — [kloppy docs](https://kloppy.pysport.org/)
- No endorsing organisations are named in the readable part of the CDF paper (author affiliations include FIFA, DFB and U.S. Soccer, which is not an endorsement); the last ~17k characters of the HTML were not read.
- Latest release / licence for statsbombpy, floodlight, databallpy, unravelsports, ScraperFC and idsse-data could not be read from the GitHub API (rate limit); only README-level facts are given for them.

## 2. xG: which open-source models exist, what features do they need, what validation is published, and what is the minimum viable xG for approximate shot locations?

### Takeaway
A logistic regression on shot distance and angle plus a body-part dummy is within about 0.0001–0.0002 Brier of richer models in KU Leuven's published benchmarks (Brier ≈ 0.078, AUROC ≈ 0.77), so a location-only xG is a defensible minimum; ML-KULeuven/soccer_xg ships pretrained open-play pipelines and its example trains on the CC BY 4.0 Wyscout dataset. Per-shot values are sensitive to location error of a couple of metres, especially close to goal, so a prototype with rough calibration should present xG as an aggregate estimate.

### Cited Findings
- **soccer_xg (ML-KULeuven).** Python package for training and analysing xG models on event-stream data in SPADL format (Opta, Wyscout, StatsBomb). Four pretrained pipelines load via `XGModel.load_model()`: `openplay_logreg_basic`, `openplay_xgboost_basic`, `openplay_logreg_advanced`, `openplay_xgboost_advanced` — open-play shots only; penalties and free kicks use separate default `PenaltyXGModel` / `FreekickXGModel`. Input: SPADL HDF5 stores named `spadl-{provider}-{league}-{season}.h5`. The README example uses the `wyscout_opensource` provider for ENG/ESP/ITA/GER/FRA 2017/18. Apache-2.0, copyright DTAI KU Leuven, written by Pieter Robberechts (2020). No metrics on the README. — [ML-KULeuven/soccer_xg](https://github.com/ML-KULeuven/soccer_xg)
- soccer_xg maintenance state: no GitHub releases; last push 2024-06-04; Apache-2.0; not archived. — [GitHub API: ML-KULeuven/soccer_xg](https://api.github.com/repos/ML-KULeuven/soccer_xg)
- **Published benchmark, location encodings (Robberechts & Davis, DTAI blog).** Logistic regression models, each with a body-part dummy, trained on two seasons of the top-5 European leagues plus the Dutch league and tested on 2018/19: Distance + Angle + Distance*Angle: Brier 0.0784, AUROC 0.7676; Raw (x, y) + Distance + Angle: 0.0784 / 0.7678; Distance + Angle: 0.0785 / 0.7669; Raw (x, y) + Angle: 0.0789 / 0.7645; Raw (x, y) + Distance: 0.0791 / 0.7648; Polar coordinates: 0.0793 / 0.7635; hand-crafted zone partition (Caley shot matrix): 0.0811 / 0.7258; Raw (x, y) only: 0.0837 / 0.7235. — [DTAI Sports blog: interplay between features and models in xG](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/illustrating-the-interplay-between-features-and-models-in-xg/)
- Same post: a probability-estimation tree on raw x/y reaches Brier 0.0787 / AUROC 0.7638 but only with enough data; a logistic regression with the advanced feature set trained on 12 seasons reaches Brier 0.0783. The authors conclude a simple logistic regression with well-constructed location features performs only slightly worse than a much richer feature set with boosted trees. — [DTAI Sports blog: interplay between features and models in xG](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/illustrating-the-interplay-between-features-and-models-in-xg/)
- **Published benchmark, data quality (DTAI blog).** Gradient-boosted model with advanced features, tested on 2018/19 top-5 leagues: Provider A Brier 0.07840 / AUROC 0.7962; Provider B 0.08020 / 0.7778; equal-weight average of the two 0.07770 / 0.7986; optimal weighted average 0.07759 / 0.8012. Roughly five seasons of training data are needed for both providers. — [DTAI Sports blog: how data quality affects xG](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/how-data-quality-affects-xg/)
- Same post, location error: shot locations for the same shot can differ by up to a couple of metres between providers; per-shot xG can differ significantly, especially at close range, while aggregates partly offset. Summed team xG pointed to a different "xG winner" in 238 games (13% of 2018/19 top-5 league games). Example season totals: Aubameyang 20.48 (A) vs 17.77 (B); Agüero 19.14 (A) vs 20.62 (B). — [DTAI Sports blog: how data quality affects xG](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/how-data-quality-affects-xg/)
- **StatsBomb embedded xG.** StatsBomb event tables loaded through statsbombpy include `shot_statsbomb_xg` and `shot_statsbomb_xg2`; the README does not explain how they are computed. — [statsbomb/statsbombpy](https://github.com/statsbomb/statsbombpy)
- StatsBomb open data may not be commercially exploited, and that restriction covers analysis derived from it (see section 4). — [StatsBomb Public Data User Agreement (LICENSE.pdf)](https://github.com/statsbomb/open-data/blob/master/LICENSE.pdf)
- **databallpy** includes "simple xG models" and an xT model alongside tracking features; MIT. — [Alek050/databallpy](https://github.com/Alek050/databallpy)
- **Understat.** soccerdata describes Understat as providing xG, xGBuildup and xGChain plus shot events with associated xG values for top European leagues. — [soccerdata: data sources](https://soccerdata.readthedocs.io/en/latest/datasources/)
- A third-party scraper listing describes Understat xG values as the output of Understat's own neural-network model, with the model and training data not published. — [Apify listing (third-party, low authority)](https://apify.com/constructive_calm/understat-football-analytics.md)
- `understatAPI` (collinb9) is MIT-licensed code, last push 2026-02-21; the code licence does not license Understat's data. — [GitHub API: collinb9/understatAPI](https://api.github.com/repos/collinb9/understatAPI)
- **penaltyblog** (MIT, v1.13.1 published 2026-10-05) offers MatchFlow (lazy pipeline over nested JSON event data), StatsBomb and Opta access, scrapers for Understat, Club Elo and FPL, goal models (Poisson, Dixon-Coles, Bayesian), ratings (Elo, Massey, Colley, Pi) and pitch plotting; its README lists xT from event data but no xG model of its own. Docs include recipes for xG per player and cumulative xG over time using StatsBomb data. — [martineastwood/penaltyblog](https://github.com/martineastwood/penaltyblog); [GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/martineastwood/penaltyblog); [penaltyblog recipe: cumulative xG](https://penaltyblog.readthedocs.io/en/latest/matchflow_recipes/04_cumulative_xg_by_time.html)
- **CDF** reserves optional per-event metric fields `xg` and `post_shot_xg`, so a home-grown xG value has a standard slot. — [arXiv:2505.15820v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15820v4)
- Opta describes its xG as a 0–1 likelihood built from nearly one million historical shots (proprietary; no open weights). — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable xG for the prototype: per shot, (x, y) in metres → distance to goal centre and goal-mouth angle → logistic regression, with a body-part dummy (foot/head) and a separate constant for penalties. Required event fields: shot start location, body part, set-piece flag (open play / penalty / direct free kick), outcome (goal or not). Freeze-frame data is not required for this tier.
- Cleanest licensing path: fit the coefficients on the Wyscout public dataset (CC BY 4.0, ~1,941 matches; shots ≈ 1.5% of 3.25M events, i.e. on the order of tens of thousands of shots) rather than on StatsBomb open data, whose agreement bars commercial exploitation of derived analysis. soccer_xg's own example already uses `wyscout_opensource`.
- Because benchmark error grows at close range under metre-level location noise, a prototype with rough pitch calibration should (a) display xG to one decimal at match/team level, (b) label it as an estimate, and (c) avoid per-shot ranking claims. An even coarser fallback (zone-based xG) costs measurable accuracy: the hand-crafted zone partition scored Brier 0.0811 vs 0.0785 for distance + angle.
- With no shot coordinates at all, xG cannot be computed; the only honest substitute is shots, shots on target and goals (conversion rates).

### Gaps
- No AUC/Brier/log-loss figures were found for the four pretrained soccer_xg pipelines specifically, and whether the weight files are stored in the repository was not confirmed.
- The "distance only" logistic regression figure extracted from the DTAI post (Brier 0.0785, AUROC 0.7669) is identical to the "Distance + Angle" row and may be an extraction artefact; re-check the source table before quoting it.
- Not retrieved: Soccermatics (David Sumpter) xG lesson code and its licence, generic "XGBoost on StatsBomb open data" reference repos, any project literally named "openxg", xG-from-tracking work, and the exact feature list of soccer_xg's "advanced" pipelines.
- A standard open constant for penalty xG was not sourced in this session.
- Understat publishes no terms of use that could be located; permission to reuse its xG values commercially is unconfirmed.

## 3. Possession value and progression: xT, VAEP, EPV, progressive actions, PPDA, field tilt, pass networks, sequences, turnovers — definitions and open implementations

### Takeaway
xT and VAEP have maintained-enough open implementations in socceraction (MIT), with further xT implementations in mplsoccer tutorials, penaltyblog and databallpy; a public 12 x 8 xT grid from Karun Singh is downloadable but carries no stated licence. Progressive pass/carry, PPDA and field tilt are provider-specific definitions that differ materially, and every one of them requires start and end coordinates.

### Cited Findings
- **xT definition (Karun Singh).** xT(x,y) = s(x,y)·g(x,y) + m(x,y)·Σ T[(x,y)→(z,w)]·xT(z,w), with s shot probability, g goal probability given a shot, m move probability, T the move transition matrix; solved iteratively from zero, with 4–5 iterations reported as adequate. Original grid 16 x 12 = 192 zones, fitted on all 2017/18 Premier League matches; an action's value is xT(end) − xT(start). Only a sanity check against top creators is offered as validation. — [Karun Singh: Introducing Expected Threat](https://karun.in/blog/expected-threat.html)
- A public xT grid file is served from Karun Singh's site: an 8-row x 12-column JSON array, values from 0.00638303 to 0.25745362. No licence statement accompanies the blog or the file. — [open_xt_12x8_v1.json](https://karun.in/blog/data/open_xt_12x8_v1.json); [Karun Singh blog](https://karun.in/blog/expected-threat.html)
- **socceraction.xthreat.** `ExpectedThreat(l=16, w=12)` is fitted with `fit()` on SPADL actions oriented with `play_left_to_right`; the docs state xT should only value actions that move the ball and keep possession, filtered via `xthreat.get_successful_move_actions`; `load_model` appears in the API index. — [socceraction docs: xT](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/valuing_actions/xT.html)
- **VAEP.** Action value = change in scoring probability minus change in conceding probability; labels are whether the possessing team scores / concedes within the next k = 10 actions; features come from the last three actions (type, result, body part, time, start/end coordinates, distance and angle to goal, movement dx/dy, time and space deltas, same-team possession, score state); two gradient-boosted binary classifiers (score, concede). The docs give no validation numbers and do not say pretrained models are shipped. — [socceraction docs: VAEP](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/valuing_actions/vaep.html)
- **mplsoccer** gallery includes tutorials "Expected threat" and "Improvements to expected threat", plus Pass Network, heatmaps (including Juego de Posición zones), pass sonars, Voronoi/Delaunay, shot freeze frame, pizza plots, radar and turbine charts, and bumpy charts; examples use StatsBomb data, with one FBref example. — [mplsoccer gallery](https://mplsoccer.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gallery/index.html)
- mplsoccer maintenance state: MIT; last push 2026-10-27. — [GitHub API: andrewRowlinson/mplsoccer](https://api.github.com/repos/andrewRowlinson/mplsoccer)
- **penaltyblog** lists xT computed from event data. **databallpy** lists xT, simple xG, pressure (Herold & Kempe 2022), team and player possession, Voronoi and Gaussian space occupation, Dangerous Accessible Space. — [martineastwood/penaltyblog](https://github.com/martineastwood/penaltyblog); [Alek050/databallpy](https://github.com/Alek050/databallpy)
- **unravelsports** works on tracking data only (Polars conversion, graph neural network datasets, Pressing Intensity, EFPI formation/position identification), loads via kloppy, MPL-2.0. It is not an event-metric library. — [UnravelSports/unravelsports](https://github.com/UnravelSports/unravelsports)
- **floodlight** (v0.4.0) is the package recommended by the DFL dataset paper for processing and visualisation; databallpy v0.5.3 was used there for event–tracking synchronisation. — [Bassek et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11787359/)
- **EPV in open data.** SkillCorner's open "dynamic events" CSVs carry EPV and pressure/difficulty measures on `player_possession` rows. CDF reserves optional `epv` and `xpass` event fields. — [SkillCorner/opendata](https://github.com/SkillCorner/opendata); [arXiv:2505.15820v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15820v4)
- **Progressive pass — Wyscout.** A forward pass qualifies if the ball moves towards the opponent goal by at least 30 m when start and end are in the team's own half, at least 15 m when they are in different halves, or at least 10 m when both are in the opponent half; successful if the next touch is a teammate's. — [Wyscout glossary: progressive pass](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/progressive_pass/)
- **Progressive pass / carry — Opta.** Progressive pass: a completed pass in the attacking two-thirds that moves the ball at least 25% closer to goal. Progressive carry: a carry that moves the ball more than 5 m upfield. — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)
- **Progressive pass — FBref.** In 2023 FBref changed its definition to measure progression relative to the opponent's goal line (10 yards), also counting any completed pass into the penalty area. — [Sports Reference blog, Feb 2023](https://www.sports-reference.com/blog/2023/02/fbref-progressive-carries-back-on-fbref-and-tweak-to-dribble-take-ons/)
- **PPDA.** Wyscout: opponent passes divided by defensive actions, counted only in the final 60% of the pitch. Opta: opposition passes allowed outside the pressing team's own defensive third divided by that team's defensive actions (fouls, tackles, interceptions, challenges, blocked passes) outside the same third. — [Wyscout glossary: PPDA](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/ppda/); [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)
- **Field tilt.** Stats Perform describes it as team final-third passes divided by both teams' final-third passes; another source defines it on final-third touches — the definitions are not identical. — [Stats Perform](https://www.statsperform.com/?p=16417); [sportshighlight.net (lower authority)](https://sportshighlight.net/pressing-data-ppda-territory-public-models/)
- **Sequences, possessions, turnovers — Opta.** Sequence: a passage of play by one team ended by a defensive action, stoppage or shot. Possession: one or more consecutive sequences by the same team, ending when the opposition gains control. High turnover: a possession starting in open play 40 m or less from the opponent's goal. Build-up attack: open-play sequence of 10+ passes ending in a shot or box touch. Direct attack: open-play sequence starting just inside own half with at least 50% of movement towards goal, ending in a shot or box touch. Direct speed: metres progressed towards the goal line divided by sequence time. — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)
- **Defensive event definitions — Opta.** Duel: a 50-50 contest, each duel won matched by a duel lost. Tackle: legal ground challenge dispossessing an opponent, won if the tackling side regains the ball or it goes safely out. Interception: moving into the line of an intended pass. Recovery: winning a loose ball. Take-on: attempt to beat an opponent, successful if the ball is kept. Chances created: assists plus key passes. — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)

### Inferences
- Fastest route to a displayed possession-value metric: look up each successful pass/carry start and end cell in a 12 x 8 xT grid. Because Karun Singh's file has no licence, the safer option is to fit a fresh grid with `socceraction.xthreat.ExpectedThreat` on the CC BY 4.0 Wyscout dataset and ship that grid with attribution.
- VAEP needs full SPADL sequences (all on-ball actions with coordinates and body part, three-action context) and thousands of matches of training data; it is not realistic for the prototype until events carry start and end coordinates. xT needs only start and end location of ball-moving actions.
- The prototype should pick one named progressive-pass definition and state it in the UI. Wyscout's rule is the only one found with fully explicit distance thresholds in metres; Opta's is a relative (25%) rule.
- PPDA needs only a coarse location flag (is the event outside the pressing team's defensive third / in the final 60%?). A reviewer-entered pitch third per event would be enough for PPDA, field tilt and high turnovers without full calibration.
- Pass networks need passer and receiver ids (CDF `receiver_id`) plus average positions; without coordinates a network graph can still be drawn with a non-spatial layout.

### Gaps
- No open "On-Ball Value"-equivalent implementation was located; StatsBomb's OBV is proprietary and no open clone was verified.
- Shot-creating actions are not defined in the Opta glossary page read (closest is chances created); FBref's SCA definition was not retrieved.
- VAEP validation numbers from Decroos et al. (KDD 2019) and Atomic-VAEP were not retrieved; the socceraction docs give none.
- Whether socceraction's `load_model` can read Karun Singh's JSON directly was not verified in the docs page read.
- StatsBomb's possession-adjustment formula was not retrieved (see section 6).

## 6. Team / season / opponent comparison methods in open-source dashboards: per-90, possession adjustment, percentile ranks, rolling averages, radars / pizzas, minimum-sample rules

### Takeaway
Concrete open implementations exist for the presentation layer (mplsoccer pizza, radar and bumpy charts; pandas rolling xG tutorials; penaltyblog cumulative-xG recipes), while the normalisation rules themselves (per-90, possession adjustment, percentile reference population) are conventions documented by providers rather than packaged in a library. No authoritative minimum-sample rule was sourced.

### Cited Findings
- **mplsoccer** provides pizza plots in seven documented variants (basic, colourful, comparison, comparison with varying scales, dark theme, different scales, different units), radar and turbine charts, and bumpy charts for rank-over-time comparisons. MIT. — [mplsoccer gallery](https://mplsoccer.readthedocs.io/en/latest/gallery/index.html)
- **StatsBomb radar convention.** Data points are calculated per 90 minutes, and defensive statistics are possession-adjusted (PAdj Tackles, PAdj Interceptions) to normalise for the opportunity to defend. — [StatsBomb: Understanding StatsBomb Radars](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/understanding-statsbomb-radars/)
- StatsBomb's rationale for possession adjustment is opportunity: a team with the ball cannot accumulate defensive actions. — [StatsBomb: Introducing Possession-Adjusted Player Stats](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/introducing-possession-adjusted-player-stats/)
- Wyscout documents its own PAdj method in its glossary. — [Wyscout glossary: PAdj](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/p_adj/)
- Possession adjustment is contested in public analysis. — [Northern Goals: Questioning Possession-Adjusting Metrics](https://northerngoals.substack.com/p/questioning-possession-adjusting)
- **Rolling xG.** A public tutorial builds a pandas function computing rolling xG-for and xG-against with a default 10-match window (`min_periods=0`) and their difference, recreating The Athletic-style trend charts. — [Steve A.Q., Figuring Out xG pt 3](https://steveaq.github.io/Figuring-Out-xG-pt3)
- Another public notebook uses a 3-match centred rolling mean followed by Savitzky–Golay smoothing. — [gibranium xG trendline](https://gibranium.github.io/others/xgtrendline.html)
- **penaltyblog** docs provide recipes for xG per player in a match and cumulative xG by time per team from StatsBomb data. — [penaltyblog recipe](https://penaltyblog.readthedocs.io/en/latest/matchflow_recipes/04_cumulative_xg_by_time.html)
- Soccermatics course material includes a radar-plot lesson. — [Soccermatics: radar plots](https://soccermatics.readthedocs.io/en/main/gallery/lesson3/plot_RadarPlot.html)
- **Sample-size evidence for xG-based comparison.** Teams average about 12 shots on target per game and strikers take roughly 50–200 shots per season; two providers' models disagreed on the higher-xG team in 13% of games. — [DTAI Sports blog](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/how-data-quality-affects-xg/)
- **Opta style indicators** usable as strength/problem flags: PPDA, high turnovers and shot-ending high turnovers, build-up attacks, direct attacks, direct speed, pressed sequences. — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)
- statsbombpy's season-level aggregates (`team_season_stats`, `player_season_stats`) are available to customers only, not through open data. — [statsbomb/statsbombpy](https://github.com/statsbomb/statsbombpy)

### Inferences
- Implementable pattern for the three dashboard sections: (1) compute per-match team totals from approved events; (2) normalise per 90 minutes (players) or per match (teams), with defensive counts optionally scaled to opponent possession; (3) compare own vs opponent with paired bars and a differential; (4) show season trends as rolling means (5- or 10-match window) once enough matches exist; (5) show percentile ranks only against a declared reference population.
- With one club's own data and few matches, percentile ranks have no valid reference set. Until the club has a season of its own data, the honest options are (a) own-team vs opponent within the matches reviewed, or (b) ranks against the Wyscout 2017/18 leagues clearly labelled as an external, differently-defined baseline.
- "Current vs previous season" and rolling-trend widgets strictly require history; with fewer matches than the window they should render as "insufficient matches (n of 10)" rather than a value.
- Strength / problem indicators can be rule-based on differentials (e.g. own value minus opponent value, or minus own rolling mean), which avoids needing a league table of comparators.

### Gaps
- No sourced minimum-sample rule (e.g. minimum minutes for percentile inclusion, minimum shots for xG comparisons) was found; thresholds used in public dashboards were not verified.
- StatsBomb's exact possession-adjustment formula and Wyscout's PAdj formula were not retrieved (only their existence and rationale).
- No complete open-source dashboard repository (FastAPI/React or Streamlit) implementing team/opponent/season comparison end-to-end was identified in this pass.
- mplsoccer's latest version number and release date were not confirmed (no GitHub release object returned; last push 2026-10-27).

## 7. Aggregating simple approved events without coordinates: which requested metrics are computable from (type, outcome, team, player, time) alone, and which strictly need coordinates or season history?

### Takeaway
Counts, completion rates, duel/tackle/dribble success, shot and goal tallies, turnovers by count, a pass-share possession proxy, home-vs-away splits and key-player leaderboards are all computable from the existing schema; xG, xT, VAEP, progressive passes, PPDA, field tilt, high turnovers and any zone-based threat or weakness analysis strictly require coordinates (or at least a pitch-third flag), and season trends and percentiles strictly require history.

### Cited Findings
- SPADL's result attribute is success/fail per action and its action types include pass, tackle, interception, take-on, shot, foul and clearance — the same type + outcome pairing the prototype already stores; the attributes the prototype lacks are start_x, start_y, end_x, end_y and bodypart. — [socceraction docs: SPADL](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/spadl/spadl.html)
- Opta's definitions of tackle won/lost, duel won/lost, interception, recovery and take-on success are outcome-based and contain no location condition. — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)
- Opta's sequence and possession definitions depend only on which team has the ball and on terminating events (defensive action, stoppage, shot), not on location. — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions)
- Location-dependent definitions: Opta high turnover (possession start 40 m or less from goal), Opta PPDA (outside the defensive third), Wyscout PPDA (final 60%), Opta progressive pass (attacking two-thirds, 25% closer), Wyscout progressive pass (30 / 15 / 10 m), Opta progressive carry (more than 5 m), field tilt (final-third passes). — [The Analyst: Opta definitions](https://theanalyst.com/articles/opta-football-stats-definitions); [Wyscout glossary: progressive pass](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/progressive_pass/); [Wyscout glossary: PPDA](https://dataglossary.wyscout.com/ppda/); [Stats Perform](https://www.statsperform.com/?p=16417)
- xT values an action as xT(end zone) − xT(start zone), so it needs start and end location. — [Karun Singh](https://karun.in/blog/expected-threat.html)
- VAEP features include start and end coordinates, body part and the two preceding actions. — [socceraction docs: VAEP](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/valuing_actions/vaep.html)
- The simplest benchmarked xG uses shot distance and angle plus body part (Brier 0.0785, AUROC 0.7669). — [DTAI Sports blog](https://dtai.cs.kuleuven.be/sports/blog/illustrating-the-interplay-between-features-and-models-in-xg/)
- In the Wyscout dataset, events arrive about every 3.59 ± 7.42 s and number about 1,682 per match, with passes ≈ 50% and duels ≈ 28% — a reference for how complete a manually reviewed event stream is. — [Pappalardo et al. 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6817871/)

### Inferences
Computable now from (type, outcome, team, player, timestamp):
- Match analysis: passes attempted / completed / completion %, tackles and tackle success, interceptions, dribbles (take-ons) and success %, shots, shots on target (if outcome encodes it), goals, conversion %, duels won/lost, fouls, clearances; per-team and per-player; by half or 15-minute bin (needs period or kickoff offsets).
- Possession (proxy): share of completed passes or of on-ball events per team; possession sequences/chains by grouping consecutive same-team events; passes per sequence; sequences ending in a shot. True time-based possession needs ball-in-play timing.
- Turnovers (count): failed passes, failed take-ons, lost duels, bad touches, plus opponent interceptions/tackles won; turnover rate per 100 possessions-proxy.
- Home vs away / own vs opponent: any of the above as paired values and differentials.
- Key players: per-player counts and success rates; per-90 once minutes played are known (needs lineups and substitution times).
- Shot-creating / key passes: derivable if the event immediately before a shot by the same team is a completed pass (sequence logic only).
- Pass network edges: only if a receiver is recorded or can be inferred from the next same-team event.

Strictly require coordinates (or at minimum a reviewer-entered pitch zone):
- xG and opponent xG (shot location; body part and situation improve it).
- xT / VAEP / any possession-value metric; progressive passes and carries; passes into final third / box.
- PPDA, field tilt, high turnovers, turnovers by zone, defensive-weakness maps, attacking-threat maps, shot maps, heatmaps, spatial pass networks.
- A pitch-third or 18-zone tag per event is sufficient for PPDA, field tilt, high turnovers and final-third entries, and a 12 x 8 cell tag is sufficient for a grid xT lookup; metre-accurate coordinates are needed only for xG and distance-based progressive rules.

Strictly require history:
- Season statistics, current-vs-previous-season trends, rolling averages, opponent trends, percentile ranks and any strengths/problem indicator defined relative to a baseline. With a single reviewed match, only within-match own-vs-opponent comparisons are valid.

Additional fields with the best cost/benefit for the review tool: period, match clock, pitch zone (then x/y), end zone for passes, body part and situation for shots, receiver, and minutes played per player.

### Gaps
- No published study was found quantifying how accurate zone-tagged (rather than coordinate) events are for PPDA, field tilt or xT; the sufficiency claims above are reasoning from the definitions.
- No source was found validating pass-share possession proxies against timed possession.
- The prototype's actual event type vocabulary and outcome encoding were not inspected in this research pass, so the mapping to SPADL types is assumed rather than verified against the codebase.
